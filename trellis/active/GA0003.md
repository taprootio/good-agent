# GA0003 — Write the write-task and work-task playbooks with the review loop

## Scope

Per [docs/design.md](../../docs/design.md) section 12, "What good-agent
ships", items 1 to 3. Source material: Taproot's
`docs/playbooks/work-task.md` and `scripts/review-exchange/README.md`, and
Trellis's `trellis/playbooks/`.

- `skill/write-task/SKILL.md`: review the problem against the codebase and
  the backlog; write the story (one-line summary, Scope with checkable
  acceptance criteria, Notes with decisions and rejected alternatives, Risks,
  dependencies, effort); review it internally with the story prompt until
  clean; review it externally with the other AI tool until clean; finalize
  into the backlog (regenerate when Trellis is present).
- `skill/work-task/SKILL.md`: ground in the repo's canonical file; read and
  verify the task; plan with commit checkpoints; review the plan internally,
  then externally; branch; choose the least-cost runtime that meets the
  quality floor for each slice (generalize Taproot's "select a runtime
  deliberately" and "review cost ceiling" without naming models); implement;
  review the work internally until clean, then externally until clean; close
  out; hand off without pushing.
- `skill/review-loop.md` (referenced by both, not a skill of its own): fresh
  context, read-only, factual prompt built from the task and the diff and
  never from the author's summary, severity semantics (`blocker`, `warning`,
  `nit`), confirm every finding against the cited code before fixing, fix,
  repeat with the whole current diff and the prior findings until `[]`,
  caps (five internal passes then a focused confirmation; external rounds
  capped per direction), design review on non-convergence, and what goes
  into the durable record: independence, round count, findings and
  dispositions, never a model or vendor name.
- The prompts the loop uses, as files the runner (GA0004) and a human can
  both read: `prompts/review-diff.md`, `prompts/review-story.md`,
  `prompts/review-plan.md`, `prompts/review-design.md`, each with the
  "close the room for hedging" rules from Taproot's section 8 and the final
  JSON block contract.
- Seam points: the playbooks name Trellis's `regenerate`, `check`,
  `branch-naming`, `gates` and `attribution` when the repo's AGENTS.md has a
  Loop contract, and two of good-agent's own, `external-reviewer` and
  `review-ceiling`, with the AGENTS.md block that declares them. Without
  Trellis the playbooks still work; backlog steps become "record it where
  this repo keeps tasks".
- This repo adopts them: its AGENTS.md Loop contract gains the two seam
  points, and its Trellis `work-task` playbook's review steps point at
  `review-loop.md`.

## Acceptance

- Each playbook is under 250 lines and passes the same front-matter test as
  GA0002's skill.
- A dry read-through by a fresh context, given only the playbook and a
  sample task, produces the loop's steps in order with no step that depends
  on Taproot-specific tooling (`verify-changed.sh`, `TRxxxxx`, Tilt).
- `review-loop.md` states every prohibition explicitly (no edits, no
  state-changing commands, no questions in place of findings, no scope
  expansion) and requires the fenced JSON array as the final block even when
  empty.

## Notes

- Taproot's playbook carries Taproot policy (signed verification, computer
  use, creator surfaces, Postgres categories) that stays in Taproot. Only the
  loop moves.
- The split with Trellis (design 12, "Overlap with Trellis"): Trellis owns the
  backlog lifecycle, good-agent owns the loop. Changing Trellis's own
  playbooks is a separate decision and not in this task.

## Risks

- Generalizing the runtime-selection language without model names can make
  it vague. Keep the rule shape ("balanced, current generation, high effort
  for review; premium only on request") and put the concrete values in the
  seam point.
